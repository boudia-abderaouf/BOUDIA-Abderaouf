Problem

Assessing well integrity means detecting corrosion in ultrasonic borehole images. The imaging tools measure pipe radius and thickness; corrosion appears as metal loss on the inner or outer wall. The difficulty is that pipes are moulded, so they carry homogeneous manufacturing patterns and collars that look, to a segmentation model, exactly like the defects it is supposed to find.

Labelling this data is therefore slow, expensive and requires a domain expert — which is precisely why there is not enough of it to train a supervised model. Interactive segmentation breaks the loop: an expert supplies a few clicks and the model does the rest, so annotation gets cheap enough to bootstrap the dataset.

The obstacle: SAM performs poorly on this domain, and WeSAM — the state-of-the-art weakly-supervised adaptation of SAM — improved it only marginally.

Background

Two research threads meet here. Interactive segmentation guides a model with sparse human input — clicks, boxes, text — rather than dense masks. Source-free domain adaptation adapts a pre-trained model to a target domain without access to the source data, which is often restricted for privacy, storage or confidentiality reasons. WeSAM applies this to SAM with a self-training architecture.

Architecture

A third, smaller change: augmentation against a shortcut

A model trained on this data learns to rely on high contrast between groove and background. A custom contrast shift was added to the strong-augmentation set specifically to create overlap between the two, removing the shortcut. Augmentation here removes a bias rather than just adding variance — and the ablation shows it contributes at every click budget.

Dataset

THBK (Azimuthal Variation of Pipe Thickness) — 180 grayscale ultrasonic images with binary masks, targeting axial long defects (“grooves”). Acquisition corrupts some samples, so cleaning and pre-processing precede training. Augmentations split into weak (vertical and horizontal flips) and strong (posterisation, sharpening, random brightness and contrast, plus the customised contrast shift).

Results

IoU as a function of the number of user clicks. The first contribution alone improves on WeSAM; the combined approach surpasses SegNext at every click count. The abstract reports a gain of over 13 IoU points.

Ablation on the customised contrast shift confirms a consistent improvement at every budget — 65.34 against 60.03 at ten clicks, 83.95 against 81.00 at twenty. Qualitatively, the approach reduces false positives more effectively than SegNext, which is what matters when the background is a manufacturing pattern that mimics the defect.

My technical contribution

Stated separately from bibliographic authorship. The author list is Imane Baho*, Eya Ghamgui*, Abderaouf Boudia, Franco Marchesoni, Josselin Kherroubi — I am third author; the first two are marked in the paper as having contributed equally.

I implemented the adaptation codebase used to produce the reported experiments:

  • The iterative click simulator — click state, computation of the largest error region between prediction and ground truth, and placement of the next click at its centroid (the paper's first contribution).
  • The two adaptation procedures: the error-driven iterative clicking procedure, and the random positive/negative point baseline it is compared against.
  • The self-training loop — anchor, student and teacher forward passes over weak and strong augmentations, with focal, Dice, IoU, anchor-regularisation and contrastive losses, and LoRA fine-tuning of the SAM backbone.
  • Dataset adapters for the evaluation corpora, and the inference, evaluation and plotting tooling that produced the click-budget curves and qualitative comparisons.

The work builds on the publicly released WeSAM codebase (Zhang et al., CVPR 2024); the contributions above are implemented on top of it.

What I took from it

  • How you simulate the human is part of the method.
  • Prompt representation is architecture. Moving from sparse to dense encoding, and feeding the previous mask back in, is where the large gain came from.
  • Augmentation can remove a shortcut, not just add variance.
