Verifiable Document Intelligence for Building Energy Audits

In production

A layered LLM reading pipeline that turns technical audit PDFs into a verifiable building model. Exhaustive page coverage replaces a single top-K search; equipment is inventoried with per-machine identity; retrieval is hybrid — dense, full-text and literal, rank-fused and deepened only where a shallow pass failed.

The core idea: a citation is not evidence — the corpus is. Every value is checked against the document that supposedly contains it, down to whether the sentence describes the building's current state or a retrofit recommendation. Units are converted in deterministic code, not by the model, and each stored value keeps its source expression and the rule that produced it. Failures are attributed to the layer that caused them.

~11k lines, ~3.4k of them tests, on a path that previously had none. Reads a 33-page audit in about 2 minutes for roughly $0.10.

Annual Building Energy Consumption Modeling

In production

A learned stand-in for dynamic thermal simulation. Training data does not exist in the wild, so it is manufactured: Monte-Carlo sampling of a plausible building population, orchestrated physics simulation, result collection, then per-climate-zone neural regressors — milliseconds instead of minutes per building.

Dataset and training versions are tracked separately, so retraining on an existing corpus and regenerating data for an unchanged trainer are both first-class operations. Releases are snapshotted into a local registry and every release produces an automatic comparison against the previous one — Markdown, CSV, JSON and a PDF of prediction scatters. Evaluation goes past R² to in-margin rates and per-chunk bias, because an aggregate score hides exactly the failure that matters.

Deep Learning for Hourly Building Energy Forecasting

Ongoing research

An annual figure ranks buildings; it does not let you reason about them. Peak load, seasonal behaviour and the hour a retrofit actually bites all live in the hourly profile. This predicts hourly heating and cooling power from building characteristics, operating scenarios and weather.

Splits are stratified at the building level, never by row — a random row split leaks the same building across splits and produces a meaningless score. Training samples 168-hour windows at random offsets inside a season block, uses Huber loss against heavy-tailed hourly power, and runs in mixed precision with resumable checkpoints. LSTM and gradient-boosting baselines were implemented and evaluated against the production regressor. Results are reported both pointwise and per building, because a model can be right by the hour and wrong by the season.

National Building Data Platform

In production

France publishes an authoritative national building database — geometry, addresses, parcels, ownership, energy certificates — and in raw form it is unusable: dozens of interrelated tables at a volume that makes national-scale joins impractical.

A dbt project turns it into analysis-ready facts: staging → intermediate → marts, with the département as the unit of build (~95 runs) so every build stays bounded and independently re-runnable. GiST spatial indexes are created in post-hooks — an unindexed geometry column makes the table pointless — source declarations are generated rather than hand-maintained, session timeouts guard every model, and tests assert the grain and the referential contract.

Fall Risk Assessment Using Gait Analysis

Research · submitted

Clinical fall-risk instruments are accurate and impractical; most published sensor alternatives buy accuracy with ten body-mounted sensors or a network that cannot run on a wearable. This study constrains itself to a single lower-back accelerometer and measures the accuracy/cost trade-off explicitly.

Four published gait-cycle segmentation algorithms were implemented and compared, per-cycle statistical and spatio-temporal features extracted, PCA applied at 99 % retained variance, and eight classifiers benchmarked on 71 older adults from the public PhysioNet LTMM database. KNN reaches 89.7 % test accuracy in 0.008 s — statistically level with a deep network 3,600× slower.

Enhanced Interactive Segmentation for Borehole Images

Published · IGARSS 2025

Detecting corrosion in ultrasonic borehole images is hard because moulded pipes carry manufacturing patterns that look exactly like the defects. Labelling needs a domain expert, which is why there is never enough of it — and SAM, plus its weakly-supervised adaptation WeSAM, both underperform on the domain.

The paper improves WeSAM two ways: error-driven click simulation — each new click placed at the centroid of the largest error region, the way a human annotator actually corrects — and SegNext-style dense prompt encoding, feeding the previous mask back so each interaction refines the last. Reported gain: over 13 IoU points.

I implemented the adaptation codebase used for the reported experiments: the click simulator, both adaptation procedures, the anchor/student/teacher self-training loop with LoRA fine-tuning, the dataset adapters and the evaluation tooling.

About

Short technical biography

I started in electronics engineering at École Nationale Polytechnique d'Alger, which is where the signal-processing habits came from, and moved into machine learning and deep learning from 2022 — first embedded, with a 1D CNN for fall detection running on a microcontroller, then into gait analysis and classical machine learning at CDTA.

A master's at Télécom SudParis (Université Paris-Saclay) took that into computer vision and foundation models: adapting SAM to industrial data at SLB, which became an IGARSS 2025 paper, and a short research stint on sparse dimensionality reduction for genomic data.

Since March 2025 I have been an AI Engineer at Cedrus Solutions, working on building energy: LLM pipelines that read technical audits into structured, verifiable models; machine-learning surrogates for thermal simulation at annual and hourly resolution; and the analytics engineering underneath.

The thread through all of it is the same: I care less about a model that scores well than about a system that can tell you why it was wrong. Most of what I build is the measurement around the model — evidence gates, per-layer failure attribution, versioned datasets, comparison reports — because that is what makes the model usable by someone who is not me.
